Timeline of Key Events

1997
Debentures created at Vale's privatization. Designed to give original shareholders economic exposure to future mineral resource growth. Iron ore royalty embedded in the instrument from day one.
1997 – 2020
Debentures held primarily by BNDES and the Brazilian government. Royalty payments flow to Brazilian sovereign holders while Vale expands its Northern and Southeastern iron ore systems.
April 16, 2021
Franco-Nevada acquires 14.7% of outstanding debentures for $538M via secondary offering. First Royalty payment (H1 2021) estimated at $28M — paid Sep 30, 2021. Initial annualized cash yield: ~10%.
2021 – 2022
Northern System contributing. Southeastern System ramping capacity from 77 Mt back to 101 Mt. Revenue begins flowing but SE System not yet over threshold. Iron ore contributes ~5-6% of FNV total revenue.
2022 – 2023
Iron ore prices moderate from 2021 highs. Royalty revenue reflects price sensitivity — Vale royalty remains one of the larger non-precious diversified contributors. SE System threshold (1.7 Bt) not yet reached.
2024
FNV guidance states SE System threshold expected to be reached in latter part of 2025. Northern System benefits from strong S11D production. Revenue from Vale royalty "relatively consistent" with prior year per Q4 2024 report.
Mid-2025 (est.)
Southeastern System contributions commence — a 35% uplift in attributable production expected once threshold is crossed. Represents a step-change in royalty economics.

Timeline of Key Events

Pre-2013
Old Franco-Nevada had royalty exposure to the Kirkland Lake camp dating to the 1980s–90s. Pierre Lassonde and Seymour Schulich specifically targeted Timmins and Kirkland Lake as premier Ontario gold camps. These legacy royalties were folded into the Newmont portfolio and re-inherited at IPO.
Oct 31, 2013
$50M NSR financing royalty signed with Kirkland Lake Gold. KL used proceeds to expand Macassa production capacity toward 2,200 t/day target. Original rate: 2.5% NSR. FNV's Harquail: "Great camps tend to keep on giving."
2013 – 2016
Macassa ramping production. Gold prices in $1,100–1,300 range. Royalty begins flowing immediately post-close. NPI component subject to capital recoupment — NPI likely minimal in early years given Macassa's capital-intensive underground expansion.
~2016 – 2017
Estimated NSR breakeven reached on the $50M cost base given Macassa's ~200,000+ oz production profile and gold at ~$1,200/oz. NSR component likely cumulative-positive by end of 2017.
2016 – 2019
Kirkland Lake Gold under Eric Sprott dramatically improved Macassa's high-grade mining discipline. Royalty rates expand with gold prices — sliding scale NSR becomes increasingly valuable as gold moves toward $1,400–1,500/oz.
Feb 2022
Agnico Eagle completes merger with Kirkland Lake Gold. Macassa absorbed into Agnico Eagle's portfolio. FNV royalty transfers to new operator. Agnico focused on expanding Macassa via Shaft #4 to 1,650 t/day.
Late 2022 – 2023
Shaft #4 completed plus new ventilation system. Macassa mill targeting full capacity of 1,650 t/day. Macassa achieves record throughput in Q4 2024. NPI component now more material with reduced capital overhang.
Q4 2024
Agnico reports Macassa achieves record quarterly throughput and gold production driven by Shaft #4 benefits and new ventilation. AK deposit contributions commencing. FNV royalty at peak earning power to date.
2025+
New paste plant commissioning (Q3 2025). AK deposit contributions scaling. Record gold prices >$2,500/oz push NSR to higher end of sliding scale. FNV's Kirkland Lake royalty increasingly significant contributor.

Royalty Nuances
Sliding Scale NSR
As gold price rises, FNV's percentage of revenue increases. At today's $3,000+/oz, FNV is likely near the 5.5% upper bound — dramatically better than the 1.5% floor.
20% NPI
Net profit royalty depends on Macassa's operating margins. With AISC ~$1,400–1,600/oz and gold at $3,000+, margins are large — NPI now a meaningful adder on top of NSR.
Operator Change
Agnico Eagle (2022 onward) is a significantly better-capitalized operator than Kirkland Lake Gold. Mine plan de-risked; Shaft #4 completed, AK satellite deposit adding production.

Key Structural Differences

Scale vs. Simplicity
Vale is a $538M macro bet on iron ore fundamentals, priced like an infrastructure yield. Kirkland Lake was a classic FNV exploration financing royalty — $50M to a mid-tier miner in exchange for perpetual upside on one of the world's best gold camps. Completely different thesis, both consistent with FNV's playbook.
NSR vs. Debenture Structure
Kirkland's sliding NSR + NPI is a direct royalty interest — FNV earns more per ounce as gold prices rise. The Vale debenture is a fixed-rate net sales royalty on gross revenue, paid semi-annually, more like a bond with commodity-linked coupons and no maturity. Neither is "better" — they serve different portfolio functions.
Return Profile
Kirkland Lake at ~272% return on cost (NSR only) in 11 years is an exemplary royalty. Vale at ~48% in 4 years is tracking similarly but at a much larger scale — once the SE System unlocks, the pace accelerates materially. Both demonstrate the FNV model: spend when others won't, collect for decades.
